## 1. Why (the data)

- Playgama telemetry, 8 Oct to 7 Nov 2026 (live build 1.4.x, the OLD game, so use it for the shape, not the exact numbers): 342 users, 288 started a level, 170 finished one; about 22 reached chapter 2, 4 reached chapter 3 and 1 reached chapter 4. The median session lasts 60 seconds (average 6 minutes).
- So almost everything is decided in the first 10 to 15 levels. A shorter campaign helps, but the order in which the player discovers things matters more.
- Today: chicken arrives at level 57, steak at 61, salmon at 64; the seven most repeated ingredients fill 32 to 43 of 148 levels; levels 89 to 101 and 124 to 148 introduce nothing new.

## 3. The design rules (checked by the script)

1. A new ingredient or a new cut at least every 4 levels up to level 50, and at least every 7 levels after that (the cuisines run out of unseen produce; the longest gaps are marked in section 4). The last 13 levels (Fusion and the Grand Finale) are mastery: no new ingredient, only new combinations.
2. A cut is taught in a level of its own before the harder dishes use it; no level teaches more than 2 new cuts.
3. Prefer a party of 2 when two dishes truly share ingredients; a party of 3 only for milestone services; shared prep ("batch") only when the dishes really share a cut.
4. 3 to 6 ingredients per dish, and about 3 to 7 prep actions per dish.
5. No near-identical dish just to fill a level. Dishes that lose their level stay in the Menu and the daily orders.
6. Each chapter ends with one service that combines the chapter's skills.

## 8. Peeling (your second request)

Today the engine FORCES a peel before any cut on garlic, onion, potato, ginger, apple, pear, pumpkin, turnip, pineapple and watermelon, so every dish that uses them carries a peel step (garlic 44 dishes, onion 39). Cucumber, beetroot, sweet potato, fennel, pea pod, mango, kiwi, pomegranate and coconut are "decoupled": peel and cut are independent, so a dish can ask for a peel or not. No dish asks for a cucumber peel today.

In this plan, levels with a peel step: onion 25, garlic 28, ginger 11, potato 5, and the fruit and root peels (apple 3, mango 3, pineapple 6, kiwi 2, watermelon 2, pear 3, turnip 3, beetroot 2, sweet potato 3, pumpkin 1).

Proposal ("peel only if the dish needs it"):
1. Make every peelable ingredient decoupled, so a dish decides. Today that is an engine rule (`peelDecoupled` in `definitions.ts`), and the skin shows on the cut faces of an unpeeled onion or garlic, which must be checked visually before we rely on it.
2. Keep the peel as a dish step where it is the point: onion rings and French onion, rock-minced garlic and pastes, potato dishes, every fruit and root peel.
3. Drop the peel where it only repeats: garlic that is smashed or sliced into a salsa, bruschetta or stir-fry, and onion chopped into a base. Those dishes keep the cut and lose the repeated peel.
4. Add 2 to 3 small new dishes where the peel is the satisfying part (cucumber ribbons, peeled apple and pear). They fit in the spare slots (this plan uses 91 of at most 100).

## 9. How it was built

- **Data and numbering:** `campaignPlan.ts` holds the levels (the first level in each `from` list gives the new level its rewards, unlock items and story); `campaignSelection.ts` maps the three numberings and the thresholds; `levelDefinitions.ts` builds LEVELS from the plan.
- **Thresholds:** by PROGRESS for systems, the menu curve, the day, staff, supplies and story; by CONTENT for cuisines (each opens with its chapter, the Asian Chef with the wok chapter), the ribeye and the steak knife.
- **Saves:** campaignVersion 4; section 6 is the migration table (a merged level asks for all of its parts; cut levels owe nothing).
- **Economy:** level pay re-tuned (x1.65 at the former-campaign position) to the same completion total; frozen baseline re-recorded (revenue 51,560, completion 243,953, COGS 11,337, quality 1,047, net 285,223); the completionist ends near $299k.
- **Tools, parties, tests:** the first shopping list at L10 is bigger; a party serves different dishes; about 45 QA suites were re-baselined and the e2e seeds re-pointed (HANDOFF lists them).
