Chef Pride

How KnifeCraft makes players feel skilled, recognised and a little bit competitive, while it stays a cozy restaurant game. Every phase: what it is, how it will look, how we build it, what we expect, how we measure it, and what could go wrong.
KnifeCraft · Playgama edition
Written 23 November 2026 · built on the developer's six ideas and the Codex plan
Status: Phases 0 and 1 are built (not yet committed). Phases 2 to 6 are planned. Phase 7 (measuring) runs alongside.
All screens in this document are drawings of the planned design, not screenshots, unless it says so.

1. The idea in plain words

Young players come back to a game when it makes them feel good about themselves and gives them something to prove. This is often called "touching the ego". It works, and it is easy to do badly. This plan does it the honest way: the game tells the player true things about their own skill, gives them a rival with a personality, and offers optional challenges. It never insults, never lies with numbers and never punishes a missed day.

Three feelings we are building

FeelingWhat the player thinksWhere it lives in the game
I am skilled"I cut cleaner than I did last week, and the game noticed."Knife Report, Level Complete card, titles, dares
Look what I built"My restaurant is getting famous."Prestige tiers, trophies, restaurant name plaque
I can be the best"I want to beat that rival chef."Named rival chefs, city ranking, weekly league, share cards

What we will not do (rules every phase must pass)

Never
  • Show a number that is not true of this player's own data. No "only 8% of chefs can do this": the game has no data on other players.
  • Insult or shame the player. Rivals tease the situation, never the person.
  • Punish a missed day or break a streak as a penalty.
  • Spam notifications or pop-ups: one message per screen visit, and Paige's "tips off" silences them.
  • Sell an advantage. Ego rewards are titles and badges, never money, and no ad is attached in the first release.
Always
  • Say exactly what happened ("Longest combo yet: 12"), with the real number.
  • Make challenges optional and never block progress.
  • Keep the cozy restaurant mood: warm words, a wink, never a harsh tone.
  • Keep copy short; longer explanations sit behind the (i) button.
  • Follow platform rules: no outside network calls, sharing only when the player taps, hidden on YouTube.

Why the order matters

Each phase builds on the one before. The first release (Phases 0 to 3) is mostly words, small saved numbers and screens we already have, so it cannot break the economy. We then measure for about two weeks before building the bigger, riskier features (prestige, league, share cards).

Limits we found (they shape the plan)

Platform and data limits
  • No real leaderboard on Playgama or CrazyGames (the two main platforms), and the game has no server. So the league is local, against the 49 rivals. It works everywhere and never shows fake "other players".
  • Sharing: the platform's share button is unsupported on Playgama, CrazyGames, Yandex and YouTube. We use the phone's own share sheet, or Save image, only when the player taps.
  • No waste per cut: the game only tracks spoilage per day. "Zero waste" therefore means a day with no spoiled stock.
  • No server clock: days and weeks are the device's own calendar. That is fine because nothing here moves money.
  • Law 4 (the plate is the score): the Knife Report shows words, not digits. Near-miss lines therefore use words only.

Phase 0 · Foundations

Built · not committed

The game's memory of the chef. Nothing new is visible yet, but every later phase reads from it.

GoalRemember the player's personal bests so the game can say true things about them. Player seesNothing directly. Phase 1 makes it visible. DataOne optional field, business.chefRecord, read through chefRecordOf(save) which clamps bad values. No new top-level save field; old saves read as zeros.

What is remembered

FieldMeaning
plates, perfectCuts, bestPerfectsPlates finished, perfect cuts ever, most perfect cuts in one plate.
bestComboThe longest cut combo of any plate.
masterful, runClean, bestRunClean, runMasterful, bestRunMasterfulMasterful plates (95+), and the current and best run of "Clean or better" (85+) and of Masterful plates in a row.
fastestMsThe fastest fair plate (at least 4 s and 3 cuts, unpaused time only).
services, bestServiceAccuracy, lastServiceAccuracy, bestServicePerfectsServices recorded, best and last average plate accuracy, most perfect cuts in a service.
seenShort markers of messages already shown, so each line appears once (kept to the last 60).

How it is built

  1. Pure rule file restaurant/chefRecord.ts: no money, no clock, no random number, no network. It only turns a finished plate or service into new numbers and into "news".
  2. Preparation screen counts perfect cuts, cuts and the unpaused cutting time during a plate, and hands them over just before the result is saved.
  3. One save: the plate rides along in the same save as its result, so there is never a second, out-of-date save.
  4. Service totals: when a first completion finishes a service, its average accuracy and perfect cuts are added.

Result and how we checked it

ResultEverything later (records, titles, dares, league) has one reliable place to read from. Zero effect on money or difficulty.
Testschef-record-qa (all pass): old saves, damaged saves, plate and service maths, "news only on a strict improvement", no money or random in the rule file, wiring.

Phase 1 · Recognition

Built · not committed

"The game knows I'm getting better." Every plate and every service says one specific, true thing about what the player did.

Before

The Knife Report shows the grade ("Clean"), a kind line, and "New Best!" with digits only when the dish's best score improves. A finished service shows pay and a new day, and nothing about how well the player cooked.

After

  • A plate a few points under the next grade says so, in words only.
  • The Level Complete card shows three tiles and, when earned, an accuracy-up line.

What we expect

  • Players see a reason to replay a plate: "one more try" when near a grade, and a visible record to chase.
  • Better players see proof of progress; weaker players are not shamed, because a line appears only for a real improvement.
  • No change to difficulty, pay or economy.

What we measure

  • record_broken events per player per day.
  • How often "Prepare Again" follows a near-miss line.
  • Services per session before and after.
Known gapsThe tiles are verified by source checks and a rendered picture, not in a full browser service (the test player cannot finish a multi-order service reliably). Please look at it in a real service.

Rules for the copy

  • At most 90 characters per line and at most one emoji (same limits Paige already has).
  • Teases the competition, never the player. A banned-word check in the QA suite enforces it.
  • No digits that claim a percentage of players.
  • Each line shows once (a marker per rival and kind).

How we build it

  1. Write rivalChefs.ts: for each rival id, a chef name and up to three lines (dare, beaten, respect). First 12 by hand, the rest from cuisine and district templates.
  2. Add a pure function rivalLine(rival, kind) that picks a line deterministically (hash of the id).
  3. Show the dare line in CityRankHero and, on tall windows, the chef's name in CityRankBadge (the Kitchen card).
  4. Extend celebrationFor with the beaten line when a rival is passed (the call site already receives the passed rivals).
  5. Add a transient Paige beat on the same no-save path her mini-game praise uses; mark it in chefRecord.seen.
  6. Wide and PC layouts: check the Progress hero, the celebration card and the Paige bubble at 1815x867 and 1280x720.
Expected resultThe ranking becomes a story: each level has a person to beat, and passing a rival is a small moment, not a number change. We expect more "one more service" behaviour near a rival level.
TestsNew rival-chefs-qa (every rival has lines, length and emoji limits, no insults, no percentage claims) and a browser test (celebration shows the reply once; Paige's message respects "Later").
RisksWriting 49 characters takes time: templates cover the gaps. Tone: the developer approves every hand-written line before release. Optional later: painted rival portraits (an art request), not required.

How it is built

  1. New pure file chefTitles.ts with the six ladders and a function that returns the identity the player leads (highest progress to the next tier; ties by a fixed order).

Rules

  • Opt-in. Nothing happens until the player taps "Take the dare". "Not today" leaves no trace.
  • One a day, chosen by a fixed hash of the calendar day among the dares the player has unlocked. Everyone sees the same dare that day.
  • No punishment. A missed or failed dare costs nothing and does not break anything.
  • Reward is a badge and a step on a "dares won" ladder (3 / 10 / 30 / 100). No money, no ad.
  • Opens at Level 6. Each dare also needs its feature to be unlocked.
  • The rival can "issue" the dare (Phase 2 voice): "Dario dares you:".

Where progress shows

On the dare row, and on the Level Complete card: "★ Dare won: No Mercy Prep".

How we build it

  1. skillChallenges.ts (pure): the dare list, each with a check function reading the chef record, the day, or the service result; plus the daily pick using the same calendar-day key the daily gift uses.
  2. State in chefRecord.challenge (day, id, accepted, progress, done); resets when the calendar day changes.
  3. Evaluate after each plate, each service, and each day close (for Zero Waste).
  4. UI: the dare row in the Next Customer details (no new screen, no layout risk) and a line on the Level Complete card.
Expected resultA reason to come back tomorrow ("what is today's dare?") without any streak pressure, and a way for skilled players to test themselves.
RisksA dare that is too easy or too hard feels flat: thresholds are tuned from Phase 7 data. "Zero Waste" depends on the stock system, so its eligibility is checked per level.

Phase 5 · Local weekly city league

After measuring

A weekly contest against the city's restaurants, with named league tiers. It is local on purpose: no server, no fake players, same rules on every platform.

LeagueChef identityMeaning
BronzeApprentice ChefNew players start here.
SilverSkilled ChefFinish the week in the top third of your group to move up.
GoldMaster Chef
PlatinumExecutive Chef
DiamondLegendary ChefThe top tier.

How a week works

  1. A week is the device's calendar week (Monday to Sunday).
  2. The player earns league points from real play: perfect cuts, services done, spotless services, and a bonus for high accuracy.
  3. Ten rival restaurants (picked from the 49 by a seeded generator for that week) get weekly points from a fixed formula scaled to the player's tier, so the contest is always fair and never impossible.
  4. On the first Kitchen visit of the new week, a result card shows the finish and any move up. Staying is the floor: no demotion shame.

How it is built

cityLeague.ts (pure) with a week key built on the daily key, the seeded generator for rivals' points, and state in chefRecord.league (week, tier, points, history). A league card in the ranking screen. The label always says "the city's restaurants", never "other players".

Phase 6 · Shareable chef cards

After measuring

A picture worth showing off: the player's record, title or league result, ready to post. It also advertises the game for free.

When a card is offered

  • A new title is earned.
  • A big record (for example 50 perfect cuts in one service).
  • A league promotion.
  • A rival is passed (with the rival's reply on the card).

How it works

  1. The existing Kitchen Moment card renderer draws the picture on a canvas (1080x1350, the game's own fonts and kitchen picture). No new library.
  2. The player taps Share: the phone's share sheet opens with the picture, or Save image if sharing is not available.
  3. The button never appears on YouTube, and nothing is ever shared automatically.

Expected result

Some players will post their achievements, which brings new players at no cost. How many is unknown: that is why we measure moment_shared before investing more.

4. Risks, and what we do about them

RiskWhat could happenHow we handle it
Tone goes wrongA line feels mean or cringe and annoys players or platform moderators.The developer approves every hand-written line; a QA suite blocks insult words and percentage claims; rivals tease situations only.
Pop-up fatigueToo many messages make the game feel naggy.One message per screen visit; gaps between rival messages; Paige's "tips off" silences them; everything is opt-in or one line.
Fake-feeling numbersPlayers distrust invented statistics.Only the player's own data; no population claims; league labelled as the city's restaurants.
Economy side effectsEgo rewards change pay or difficulty.Titles and badges are badge-only (no money); the economy test suites must stay unchanged.
Layout crowdingThe Kitchen is already tight, especially on small phones.New content goes on existing screens or hides on short windows; every phase is tested at 320x568 to 1815x867.
Platform rejectionSharing or social prompts break platform rules.Share only on tap; hidden on YouTube; no outside calls; only the Bridge touches the platform.
Wrong thresholdsTitles or dares are too easy or too hard.All thresholds are marked "proposed" and tuned from Phase 7 data before the next phase.
Engagement without revenueMore play does not mean more ad or purchase income.Phase 7 compares rewarded-ad views; the decision rule requires they do not drop.

5. Testing approach (every phase)

  • Gates: type-check, lint, formatting on changed files, build, preflight.
  • New QA suite per phase (pure rules): records, rival lines, titles, dares, league, prestige. Each checks old saves, clamping, no money/random/clock in rule files and copy safety.
  • Existing suites that must stay green: UI copy, save safety, migration, city ranking, achievements and economy baselines, assistant, layout, ads.
  • Browser tests at 320x568, 360x640, 390x844, 768x1024, 1280x720, 1815x867 and a phone on its side (740x360): nothing sideways, 48 px touch targets, nothing covering controls, no console errors.
  • Old-save check every time: a save from before the change loads and plays.
  • Commit only after the developer says so; each phase is its own commit with a note of any rule change to an old test.
