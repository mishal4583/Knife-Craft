**What is the developer's and what is mine**

- The developer's: the six machine prices.
- Mine (derived, change freely): table #2 = 0.9 x the machine price, Tier 2 = 3 x, Tier 3 = 9 x, each table 1.17 x dearer than the last. Servers $200 / $1,200 / $6,000 and
  Cleaners $600 / $3,000 (only used by the progression run; the Server must stay cheaper than Coffee's table #2, $450).

**How to read it like an idle game.** "Per hour" and "per day (24 h)" are what a station makes when it never stops (the idle-game headline number). A real player only
collects what the counter holds: **cap hours x per-hour** per visit (section 5). That is the number that decides how often they come back.

## What the numbers say

- **Coffee** is the hook: the typical player buys it the minute the floor opens, and it pays back in 8.3 hours of running (about 2 visits).
- **The ladder is flat**: single-table paybacks 8 / 12.5 / 16 / 19 / 23 / 35 hours. A new machine is always the best next buy for a few days; after that tables and tiers
  take over (section 2, last column).
- **Tier 2 is a many-tables upgrade**: one table takes 3-13 days to repay it, ten tables take 1.2-5 days. Tier 3 is a long-term sink (4-15 days at ten tables).
- **The table wall is deliberate**: each table is 17% dearer, so table #25 of Coffee costs $16,653 and #50 costs $843,595 (section 7). Nobody reaches 50 tables;
  ten is a strong late-game station.
- **Warning from the earlier work, still true**: a player who advances the campaign slowly and visits the floor regularly earns more on the floor than in the campaign
  from day 7. That is structural to any real-time income, not caused by the prices.
